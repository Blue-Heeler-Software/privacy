BlueHeeler for Android

Privacy Policy

BlueHeeler helps you find Bluetooth trackers and other devices that may be following you. People often use it when their safety is at risk, so it is built to keep what it learns on your phone. This policy explains what the app reads, what it keeps, the few times anything leaves your phone, and how you control all of it.

Who we are

BlueHeeler is published by Blue Heeler Software ("we", "us"). This policy covers the BlueHeeler app for Android, distributed on Google Play with the package name com.kosheen.antistalker.

BlueHeeler does its work on your phone. We do not operate any server that the app talks to, and the app sends us none of what it records. When this policy describes information being "kept" or "stored", it means on your own phone, by software you control.

Questions about this policy can go to xredux@protonmail.com.

What BlueHeeler reads

Signals from devices around you

Bluetooth and Wi-Fi devices announce themselves over the air so that other devices can find them. BlueHeeler listens to those announcements and reads:

  • device identifiers (Bluetooth and Wi-Fi addresses);
  • device names and Wi-Fi network names;
  • the kind of device or company a device says it is, and the services it advertises;
  • signal strength, and the time each device was heard.

BlueHeeler uses these to recognise trackers, to tell one device from another, and to notice a device that keeps turning up wherever you go. It never looks these identifiers up online and never sends them to us.

Notifications from your security apps Optional, Pro

If you turn on the Security log and give BlueHeeler Android's notification access, it reads notifications only from the security apps you tick, such as a camera, doorbell or alarm app. For each one it keeps the app's name, the notification's title and text, and the time, next to the devices it heard at that moment. That lets you see, for example, that a camera spotted motion while an unknown device was nearby. It ignores every other app. You can withdraw notification access in Android's settings at any time.

What BlueHeeler does not read

BlueHeeler does not read your contacts, messages, photos, files, browsing, call history or accounts. It does not use your phone's advertising ID. The camera is used only if you choose to scan a QR code to join your phones into a Pack, and no picture is kept.

Your location

Use location is off when you install BlueHeeler. It stays off unless you turn it on in Settings and accept Android's location permission. If you do:

  • While protection is running, BlueHeeler notes where you are when it hears devices, so it can tell whether one of them has moved with you.
  • It asks Android for a fresh, precise position only when it hears a device that matters: a tracker, a device you flagged or pinned, a name you asked it to watch for, or a device that seems to be following you. It asks at most once every two minutes.
  • Without Pro, the positions noted with devices are held in the phone's memory only, and are gone when BlueHeeler closes (apart from the rough areas described below).
  • With Pro you can turn on Places. BlueHeeler then keeps a numbered point for each place where a tracker or flagged device was heard, so you can build a dated record over time, and you can name each place. Places is off until you turn it on.
  • Spot devices you keep seeing works only while Use location is on. It keeps the rough area, to about 2 km, where everyday devices you hear often tend to stay, for up to 60 days, so BlueHeeler can offer to stop warning you about them. Trackers are never remembered this way.

Android, not BlueHeeler, works out your position. If Google Location Accuracy is on in your phone's settings, Android may use Google's location service to do so, as it does for every app on the phone. That is a setting of your phone, outside BlueHeeler.

Android 8 to 11. On these versions, Android shows Bluetooth devices only to apps that hold location permission, so BlueHeeler asks for it there simply to scan. Holding that permission does not turn on Use location. Until you confirm Use location's explanation, any position BlueHeeler sees on these versions is rounded to about 2 km.

Android backup

Android can copy app data to your Google Account, so it comes back when you replace or reset your phone. If backup is on for your phone, Android copies the records marked "Copied" in the table above: your settings and device lists, alert history, the Security log (including the text of those notifications), your Pack passphrase, your Dead Man's Switch setup, and whether Pro is unlocked. Google stores this copy under its own privacy policy. The same records may also move across when you set up a new phone from your old one.

Places, positions, familiar device areas, the scanning record and diagnostic copies are never copied to a backup or a new phone.

We keep backup available on purpose: a backup has helped people recover a record they needed after their phone was lost or reset. If you would rather not have a copy with Google, turn off backup in your phone's settings (usually Settings, Google, Backup). You can delete an existing copy from the same place or from Google Drive's Backups list.

Versions before 1.0.170. Places, Suggest an address and Play sound arrive in version 1.0.170. In earlier versions, Android backup copies all of BlueHeeler's private data except the scanning record, so it also copies the familiar device areas (rough, to about 2 km) and, if you turned them on, the diagnostic copies. Earlier versions also remind you to turn protection back on after a restart, rather than restarting it themselves. Version 1.0.168 and earlier also include a logging part of Google's billing software, which may send Google technical information about BlueHeeler's checks with Google Play, such as which billing steps ran and whether they worked, and hold the internet and network state permissions for it. Later versions leave it out and hold neither permission.

Good to know. Turn and Burn wipes BlueHeeler on your phone, not the copy held by Google. That copy is replaced the next time Android backs up BlueHeeler, which happens after you open it again. To remove it straight away, delete it in your Google Account.

Suggest an address in detail

With Pro, a place's page can show a Suggest an address button; you can hide it in Settings, Location and Places. Each time you tap it, BlueHeeler first asks whether to send that place's map point to Google. Only if you tap Send to Google does it pass that one point to Android's address lookup, which Google Play services runs, so the point goes to Google over the internet, even with Location turned off. Google Play services makes the request from your phone, so Google may keep the point and link it to your phone, under Google's privacy policy. Once you tap Send to Google, the point may reach Google even if you then tap Cancel or no answer comes back.

The answer is Android's best guess at the nearest street address. It stays a suggestion until you save it as the place's name, and every log and report marks a name that started from one. BlueHeeler never looks up an address by itself: not while recording, and not when you save, share or delete anything. It shows the button only where the phone's address lookup is Google's, or where it cannot tell. A replacement for Google Play services that presents itself as Google's, such as microG, would receive the point instead of Google.

Links in the app, such as this policy or the Google Play page, open in your browser only when you tap them. The in-app help is built in and loads nothing from the internet.

Signals BlueHeeler sends

BlueHeeler mostly listens. It sends radio signals of its own only in these cases:

  • Pack Optional, Pro lets your own phones work together over Bluetooth, with no internet and no cloud. While it is on, each phone broadcasts a heartbeat holding a random Pack ID, its compass heading and signal calibration, its Tripwire state, and up to 16 recent sightings, each made of the last half of a device's Bluetooth address with its signal strength and age. If Use location is on, the heartbeat also carries your position, rounded to about 2 km. If you turn on trust sharing, it also shares which devices you trust or labelled. Heartbeats are signed with your Pack passphrase, so a stranger cannot inject fake ones, but they are not encrypted: anyone nearby with the right equipment can read them. Leave Pack off if you do not want this on the air.
  • Play sound: when you tap it, BlueHeeler connects to that tracker and sends the command that makes it ring, and a stop command if you ask. It never pairs with the tracker.
  • Read tracker details Off unless you turn it on: connects briefly to a known tracker to read its maker, model and firmware, at most once a day per device. It reads only; the tracker may log your phone's Bluetooth address.
  • Wi-Fi scans: to see Wi-Fi devices, BlueHeeler asks Android to scan, and Android asks nearby networks to identify themselves, as it does for any Wi-Fi scan.

The version on Google Play never transmits Wi-Fi frames of its own.

Google and other companies

BlueHeeler shares nothing with advertising networks, analytics companies, data brokers or crash reporting services, and has none built in. The only company that receives anything because of BlueHeeler is Google, in the ways listed above: Google Play and its billing software, Android backup, the optional address lookup, and, if Google Location Accuracy is on in your phone, Android's location service. Google handles that information under the Google Privacy Policy, and you can review and manage much of it at myaccount.google.com.

When you share something from BlueHeeler to another app, that app's own privacy policy applies.

How your information is protected

  • BlueHeeler's records sit in its private storage, which Android keeps apart from other apps and encrypts along with the rest of your phone.
  • Places can be locked behind your fingerprint, face or screen lock. The lock is on when you start using Places, and locks again after 10 minutes without use. Reports and logs that include places ask for it too.
  • Pack heartbeats are signed (HMAC-SHA-256), so a stranger cannot inject false data. They are not encrypted, as explained above.
  • Diagnostic copies, if you turn them on, are written where other apps with file access and a USB connection can read them. Leave them off unless you need them for a bug report.
  • Anyone who can unlock your phone can open BlueHeeler. If you are worried someone has access to your phone, use the Places lock and consider Turn and Burn.

No security is perfect, and files you save or share are outside BlueHeeler's protection once they leave the app.

Your choices and deleting data

  • Turn features off at any time in Settings: Use location, Places, Spot devices you keep seeing, Pack, the Security log, Read tracker details, diagnostic copies and Suggest an address.
  • Delete selectively: one place or all places, your history, or a device from any list.
  • Choose how long history and places are kept.
  • Turn and Burn (switched on in Settings before use) stops protection, then overwrites and deletes everything BlueHeeler holds in its storage on your phone: settings, lists, history, the Security log, places, familiar device areas, the scanning record and diagnostic copies. It does not remove files you saved to Downloads, the copy in your Google backup, your Pro purchase (Google Play restores it), or a random Pack ID that means nothing on its own. Phone storage spreads writes around to protect itself, so no app can promise every trace is physically erased.
  • Uninstalling removes everything BlueHeeler stored on your phone, except files you saved to Downloads or shared. The Google backup copy is removed by Google in time, or straight away by you.

Your privacy rights

Depending on where you live, privacy law, such as Australia's Privacy Act, the GDPR in the European Union and United Kingdom, or California's privacy laws, may give you the right to see, correct, delete or take a copy of personal information about you, and to object to how it is used.

Because BlueHeeler keeps your information on your phone, you can use those rights yourself, straight away: view everything in the app, export it with Save log or a report, and delete it as described above. We hold nothing about you from the app, so a request to us would find nothing to return. We do not sell or share personal information, and we make no automated decisions about you.

For information Google holds, such as your Play purchases, backups and address lookups, use Google's tools at myaccount.google.com.

If you email us, we receive your email address and whatever you write. We use it only to reply and help, and keep it only as long as that takes. If you are unhappy with how we handled your information, please tell us first; you can also complain to your local privacy regulator, which in Australia is the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (oaic.gov.au).

Information about other people

The devices BlueHeeler hears belong to the people around you. It uses their broadcast signals only to protect you, and only on your phone. If you save or share a log or report, it may contain identifiers of other people's devices, so please share it only with those who need it, such as the police, a lawyer or a support service.

Children

BlueHeeler is made for adults and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly receive information from children, and the app sends us nothing from anyone.

Changes to this policy

When BlueHeeler changes how it handles information, we update this page and the date at the top no later than the new version reaching Google Play. Significant changes are also described in the app's release notes.
